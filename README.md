Part 3
a. Civic technology is all about using technology to improve the government - 
public relationship with communications, service - delivery, political process, 
and decision - making software. These software are built by community volunteers,
nonprofits, consultants and private companies cooperating with government. According 
to Schrock (2019) civic technology aims to increase engagement between government
and citizens, which in the U.S. grew out of two combined movements: open data/open
government and tech - based community engagement. Some examples are: online petitions,
311 service request apps, and open data portals. 
Based on the American Planning Association civic tech is placed inside a larger
smart city framework (Hurtado & Gomez, 2021). It describes smart city as one that uses
technology fairly, cares for both the environment and the people, and invites its 
residents to help shape it. In this view, 'gov.tech' helps the city government
work more efficiently, while civic tech helps residents and government work together. 
Civic tech basically helps smart cities in three main ways. Firstly, it encourages
teamwork and openness: residents and volunteers and governments share their ideas
and build software together (McNutt et al., 2018). Secondly, the focus is on people.
Experts now agree that the city is truly 'smart' if it involve residents in decisions,
not just for its own sake. Thirdly, it allows cities to improve step by step, 
by gathering data on the way residents treat the city, and make changes based on 
that feedback.
One example is Chicago's Array of Things. In 2016, the University of Chicago,
Argonne National Laboratory, and the City of Chicago installed sensor boxes on 
light poles to measure temperature, air quality, and traffic. Researchers, developers,
and city officials could use it to address problems such as, traffic safety, 
pollution, and flooding. This project also includes community meetings
to figure out the community needs and get their input on privacy rules
(Smart Chicago Collaborative, 2016). 
In conclusion, civic tech helps smart cities become more open, more inclusive 
and responsive to the residents, and not only more high - tech city.
